*Senanayake Samudraya*
![Senanayake Samudraya](img/SenanayakeSamudraya.webp)
Samudraya is a Sinhala word that means ‘the sea’. So Sri Lankans don’t casually use that word to describe a man-made tank. In fact, throughout its millennia-old history, there’re only two tanks that deserve the word – Parakrama Samudraya in Polonnaruwa and Senanayake Samudraya in the Ampara District.

The Senanayake Samudraya is a project spearheaded by the First Prime Minister of independent Sri Lanka, Mr D S Senanayake. Completed in 1953, it is one of the most iconic man-made tanks with an earthen dam in Sri Lanka. Also known as the Gal Oya Reservoir, it is the largest man-made tank in Sri Lanka. As such, it truly does resemble an inland sea when at full capacity.

When you are exploring Ampara, you definitely have to visit Inginiyagala to see this amazing tank. What makes the site even more impressive is the sight of the Inginiyagala Mountain standing as a silent guardian nearby. As one of the biggest tanks in a highly agricultural area, it provides water for irrigation for miles around. Not only that, but it also powers a small hydroelectric power station.

*Gal Oya National Park*
![Gal Oya National Park](img/GalOyaNationalPark.webp)
The catchment area of the Senanayake Samudraya enriches several sanctuaries and a national park. The national park – Gal Oya – serves as the main catchment area of the reservoir. While it is not as famous as parks like Yala, it does receive a steady stream of visitors throughout the year. What draws them to the park is its elephant population. Unlike several parks where you can only spot elephants during certain months, you can spot elephants all around the year at Gal Oya National Park.

It is also a fantastic place to spot birds. While the park only has about 40 species of mammals, it has over 150 species of birds. The vegetation within Gal Oya tends to range between forests, shrubs and grasslands. As a result, there’s plenty of room to accommodate different types of birds and their nesting habits.

*Buddhangala Monastery*
![Buddhangala Monastery](img/BuddhangalaMonastery.webp)
When you are exploring Ampara, you come to appreciate one fact above all others: its untamed wilderness. As you get further away from the town centre, the landscape gradually turns wilder until eventually, forests surround you. The Buddhangala Monastery hides away in one of these forests. As such, it is a lesser-known destination in Sri Lanka.

Researches assert that the monastery belongs to an ancient Sri Lankan kingdom that began in the fourth century BC. Therefore, while the exact age of the remnants of the temple is hard to determine, it does have a considerable history. Due to various factors, the monastery remained hidden for a long time, with the forest reclaiming its grounds thanks to neglect. It was only in 1964 that the monastery once again came to the attention of people.

Since almost no one remembers the monastery’s origins, much of it still poses several questions. For instance, during an excavation, archaeologists discovered the sacred relics of Buddha’s two main disciples. However, no one can provide an answer for how these relics came to Sri Lanka.

*Maha Oya Hot Water Springs*
![Maha Oya Hot Water Springs](img/MahaOyaHotWaterSprings.webp)
If you love hot springs, you can find some awesome ones while exploring Ampara. The Maha Oya Hot Water Springs are about an hour and a half away from Ampara town. Apparently, the island’s hottest hot water spring is among the group of seven hot water springs at Maha Oya. Most locals around the area believe that the water carries healing properties. Whether or not this is true, at over 50-degrees Celcius, the water from the hottest spring will either feel blissful or make your skin feel like it’s burning.

If the heat of that spring is too much, you can certainly try the other six springs. The coldest spring records at just over 30-degrees Celcius. The temperature of the other four springs ranges between these two temperatures. Not surprisingly, the water from the spring with the highest temperature is not suitable for bathing. In fact, out of the seven springs, only two are safe enough to bathe from!

*Japanese Peace Pagoda, Ampara*
![Japanese Peace Pagoda, Ampara](img/JapanesePeacePagoda.webp)
Ampara Peace Pagoda (also known as Ampara Sama Ceitya) is one of a number of Peace Pagodas in the world, built since World War II and designed to promote the non-violence in the community and unite them in their search for world peace. It is located in Ampara, a small town located in the Eastern Province of Sri Lanka and is one of the Ampara's most prominent landmarks.

There are another four Peace Pagodas constructed in various places around Sri Lanka. They are located at Unawatuna, Adam's Peak, Bandarawela and Walapane
The Peace pagoda temple is situated 4 km far away from the ampara DS Senanayake Rd from the clock tower, which heads towards Inginyagala.[4]
The temple consists of a Pagoda, a Mahayana style Vihara geya (Image house) and a small Bodhi tree. The pagoda is retinue with 99 small pagodas and there is a straight path from the entrance to the pagoda.
